SWEEPR Cleaner vs BleachBit

BleachBit is a respected free GPLv3 open-source cleaner with no corporate adware. Here is an honest look at when BleachBit is plenty—and where SWEEPR Cleaner adds crucial safety and modern app coverage.

QUICK VERDICT

BleachBit (created by Andrew Ziem under the GPLv3 open-source license) is completely free, ad-free, and has zero Gen Digital or corporate telemetry ties. It excels at shred / free-space wiping, headless --clean CLI scripting, and winapp2.ini rules. Why Safety Guardrails Matter More Than Raw Checkboxes

In technical communities like r/sysadmin and r/Windows11, BleachBit is rightly respected for zero corporate ties, transparent GPLv3 Python source code, and command-line --clean automation. Yet non-technical users routinely wipe saved logins or corrupt open SQLite databases by ticking unfamiliar boxes in its GTK3 sidebar. SWEEPR pairs on-demand operation (with optional lightweight tray / scheduled cleaning and Zero-Telemetry / Offline Mode) with non-touching access_mode(0) process-lock checks, headless CLI automation (--scan, --clean, --dry-run, --json, --category), local custom_rules.json validation, SSD-aware NVMe wear-leveling protection (restricting free-space zeroing to DiskKind::HDD while cleaning thumbcache_*.db and Recent\*.lnk traces safely), and a 7-Day Undo Quarantine (configurable 2–10 GB disk budget, 5 GB default, same-volume O(1) move; OS Recycle Bin fallback if file exceeds budget; excludes shredded files, browser DBs, and registry edits), separating safe regenerable caches (NVIDIA DXCache, Steam depotcache) from sensitive paths like ProgramData Package Cache. No native Linux build is shipped yet (and there is no hosted PPA or distro repo yet), though docs/public-rules-catalog.json publishes 20 Linux cache rules (116 patterns, plus .desktop/Flatpak/Snap uninstaller specs) alongside CI deb/rpm/AppImage packaging targets pending target runtime verification.
